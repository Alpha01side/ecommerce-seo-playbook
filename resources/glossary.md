# Glossary for Store Owners

Plain definitions of the terms used across this repository, written for someone who runs a shop rather than a server. Where a term is commonly misused, the misuse is noted, because that is usually more useful than the definition.

**Alt text.** The written description of an image, read aloud by screen readers and used by search engines when the image cannot be understood. Commonly misused as a place to list keywords, which helps nobody and is worse than leaving it empty.

**Anchor text.** The visible, clickable words of a link. It tells both people and search engines what to expect at the destination. "Waterproof walking boots" is anchor text; "click here" is a wasted opportunity.

**Canonical tag.** A line in the page code saying "this content's preferred address is X". It is a hint, not a command, and it is ignored when the two pages are clearly different. Useful for genuine duplicates, useless as a substitute for fixing URL structure.

**Cannibalisation.** Two or more of your own pages competing for the same search. Usually shows up as both pages ranking poorly and swapping positions. The fix is to merge them or to make each one clearly about something different.

**Category page.** A page listing products of a type. Often your highest-value page for broad search demand, and often the one with the least written content.

**Crawl.** A search engine or tool fetching your pages by following links. Crawling is not the same as indexing: something can be crawled and never indexed, or indexed without being crawled recently.

**Crawl budget.** The rough amount of crawling a site gets. It matters on large stores, where thousands of filter URLs can absorb attention that should go to new products. On a 200 product store it is rarely the real problem.

**Duplicate content.** The same or near-identical text on more than one URL, either within your store or shared with other stores. Usually not a penalty, usually a dilution: search engines pick one version and the rest earn nothing.

**Facet or filter.** A refinement such as colour, size or price. Useful for shoppers, dangerous for SEO when each combination generates its own crawlable URL.

**Impressions and clicks.** Impressions are how often your page appeared in results, clicks are how often someone chose it. Rising impressions with flat clicks almost always means the title and description need work, not the page content.

**Indexation.** Whether a URL is stored in the search engine's index and therefore eligible to appear. The number of indexed URLs compared with the number you intended is one of the most useful health metrics for a store.

**Internal link.** A link from one page of your site to another. The cheapest and most under-used SEO lever available to a store owner.

**Lazy loading.** Delaying the loading of images until they are near the viewport. Good for everything below the fold, bad for the main product image, which should load immediately.

**Meta description.** The summary text a search engine may show under your title. It is not a ranking factor in itself; it is advertising copy that influences whether anyone clicks.

**Mockup.** A generated image showing a design applied to a blank product. Standard in print-on-demand, and shared with every other seller using the same generator, which is why at least one original photograph per design matters.

**Noindex.** An instruction that a page should not be included in the index. Requires the page to be crawlable so the instruction can be read, which is why combining noindex with a robots.txt block does not work.

**Orphan page.** A page with no internal links pointing to it. New products and seasonal categories become orphans constantly.

**Pagination.** Splitting a long list across numbered pages. Should be crawlable so products are discoverable, but is not something you promote.

**Redirect (301).** A permanent instruction that a URL has moved. Should point to the closest equivalent page, not the home page, and should not chain through several hops.

**Rich result.** An enhanced search listing showing extra detail such as price or availability, made possible by structured data. Eligibility is not a guarantee, and inaccurate markup is the fastest way to lose it.

**Robots.txt.** A file telling crawlers which paths not to fetch. It controls crawling, not indexing, and it is not a security measure. Anything genuinely private needs authentication.

**Sitemap.** A file listing the URLs you believe deserve indexing. Most useful as a diagnostic when it is split by type and kept free of redirects and errors.

**Slug.** The readable part of a URL that identifies the page, such as `allotment-gardener-mug`. Short, stable and descriptive beats clever.

**Structured data.** Machine-readable markup describing what is on the page, such as product name, price and availability. Must match what the visitor sees.

**Thin content.** A page that does not give a visitor enough to make a decision. On stores this usually means a supplier paragraph, a mockup and a size chart.

**Title tag.** The clickable headline in search results and the browser tab. Written for a person who is scanning ten similar listings.

**Variable product.** A product with options such as size or colour. The main source of accidental duplicate URLs and mismatched structured data prices in WooCommerce stores.
