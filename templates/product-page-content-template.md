# Product Page Content Template

A structure for writing a product page that answers a buyer's questions in the order they ask them. Copy the outline, fill it in, delete the guidance notes, and publish.

The order matters more than the wording. People scan a product page in a predictable sequence: is this the right thing, will it fit or work, what does it cost me in money and time, and can I trust you to send it.

## Outline

### 1. Product name (H1)

The name a customer would recognise and say out loud. Include the attribute that distinguishes it from your other products: material, size, style, capacity, fit.

Avoid: internal codes, supplier names, and stacked keywords.

### 2. Opening, two to four sentences

Answer three things immediately:

- What it is, in plain words.
- Who it suits, or what situation it is for.
- What is different about this one compared with the obvious alternative.

If you cannot write a genuine third sentence, you have a product that competes on price or availability. Say that honestly instead of inventing a difference.

### 3. Key details, as a short list

Between four and eight lines, each a fact a buyer needs to decide. Typical examples: material and weight, dimensions, capacity, compatibility, power or battery, care requirements, what is included in the box.

Keep marketing language out of this block. It is the reference section and people scan it.

### 4. Sizing, fit or compatibility

The single most common reason for returns and abandoned carts. Give a real sizing reference, not just a chart: what the model is wearing, how it compares with a common standard, whether it runs small. For technical products, state exactly what it does and does not fit.

### 5. How it is used, or why it exists

Two or three sentences of context. For a designed or handmade product this is where the story belongs, briefly. For a functional product, describe the situation it solves. This is also where original wording naturally appears, which matters if your specifications are supplier-standard.

### 6. Objections, answered

List the two or three things a hesitant buyer worries about, and answer them plainly. Common ones: durability, whether the colour matches the photo, how long delivery takes, whether it needs assembly, what happens if it is wrong.

Answering an objection honestly, including saying "this is not suitable for X", builds more trust than any badge.

### 7. Delivery and returns, in one line each

Not a link to the policy. One sentence each, with real numbers. "Dispatched within two working days, delivery two to four days. Returns accepted for thirty days, unused."

### 8. Related choices

Link to the cheaper alternative, the upgrade, and the accessory that most buyers need. Use the product names as anchor text. Three good links beat a carousel of twelve.

### 9. Reviews or evidence

If you have real reviews, show them. If you do not, do not fake or imply them. Photographs from customers, a note about how many you have sold, or a clear returns promise all work as evidence instead.

## Metadata to write at the same time

**Title tag.** Lead with the product and its distinguishing attribute, then the qualifier a buyer searches with. Brand last unless the brand is the reason people search.

**Meta description.** Who it suits and what is included, written as ad copy. Not a summary of the page.

**URL slug.** Short, readable, product name only. No category path repetition, no dates, no IDs.

**Image alt text.** Describe each image for someone who cannot see it, using the words a customer would use.

## Length guidance

There is no correct word count. A cable needs eighty words. A mattress needs eight hundred. Write until the buying questions are answered and then stop. Padding a page to hit a target makes it worse, and the padding is always the first thing a reader skips.

## Before you publish

- Read the page as someone who has never seen the product.
- Check that every claim is true and you could support it.
- Check the specification block matches the actual product data in the store.
- Confirm the page does not duplicate another page of yours competing for the same search.
- Note the date and your before figures, so you can judge the rewrite later.
