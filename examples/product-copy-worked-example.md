# Worked Example: Rewriting a Product Page

A complete before and after for one product, with the reasoning at each step. The product is invented so that nothing here is taken from a real store, but the problems are the ones I see most often.

**The product:** a ceramic mug sold by a small print-on-demand store, printed with a design aimed at allotment gardeners.

## Before

**Title tag:** Mug 11oz White Ceramic - MyStore

**H1:** Product 4471

**URL:** /shop/product-4471/

**Description on the page:**

"High quality 11oz ceramic mug. Dishwasher and microwave safe. Printed using sublimation for a long lasting finish. Perfect gift for any occasion. Order yours today!"

**Images:** three supplier mockups, files named mockup-1.jpg, mockup-2.jpg, mockup-3.jpg, all with alt text "mug".

### What is wrong with it

The title and H1 describe a blank mug, not this mug, so the page competes with every other white mug on the internet and with the seller's own forty other designs. The URL carries an internal ID that means nothing to anyone. The description is the supplier's text, word for word, shared with thousands of other stores. "Perfect gift for any occasion" says nothing; a mug for allotment gardeners is not a gift for any occasion, it is a gift for one fairly specific person. There is no mention of what the design actually says, no capacity comparison a coffee drinker would want, and the images tell a search engine nothing.

Note what is not wrong: the specification facts are accurate and worth keeping. The problem is that they are the only content.

## After

**Title tag:** Allotment Gardener Mug, 11oz Ceramic Coffee Mug

**H1:** Allotment Gardener Mug

**URL:** /shop/allotment-gardener-mug/

**Description on the page:**

Opening:

"A sturdy 11oz mug for the person who checks the weather forecast before they check their phone messages. The design is a hand-drawn allotment plan, with the beds labelled the way most people actually label them: 'spuds', 'the bit that never works', and 'tomatoes, probably'. It was drawn for my own plot before I put it on a mug."

Key details:

- 11oz (approximately 330ml), which is a large tea or a standard mug of filter coffee
- White ceramic with a glossy finish, printed both sides so it reads the same for left and right handed drinkers
- Dishwasher and microwave safe
- Height 9.5cm, diameter 8.2cm, fits under most single-serve coffee machines

Context and objections:

"The print is sublimated, which means the design is in the glaze rather than sitting on top of it, so it will not peel at the handle like a transfer print does. It will fade slowly over years of dishwasher use, as all printed mugs do. If you want something for daily site use that will be dropped on concrete, this is the wrong mug, and an enamel one is the right answer."

Delivery and returns:

"Printed to order and dispatched within three working days. Delivery two to four days after that. Returns accepted for thirty days if unused, and I pay the return postage if the print is faulty."

**Images:** the main mockup renamed allotment-gardener-mug-front.jpg with alt text "White ceramic mug printed with a hand-drawn allotment plan, front view", plus one new photograph of the mug on a potting bench for scale and context.

### Why each change was made

The title now matches something a person would actually type, and leads with the design rather than the blank. The H1 matches the title without duplicating it awkwardly, and the URL is readable and stable.

The opening paragraph cannot be copied from a supplier, because it is about this design and this seller. That single paragraph does more for differentiation than any amount of keyword placement.

The key details answer the questions that stop people buying a mug: how big is it really, will it fit the machine, does the design show on both sides. The capacity is translated into something meaningful, because almost nobody knows what 11oz looks like.

The objections section does something counter-intuitive: it tells some visitors not to buy. That costs a small number of sales and prevents a larger number of returns and poor reviews, and it makes every other claim on the page more believable.

Delivery times are stated with real numbers, because "fast shipping" is not information.

The images now describe the product and the design. The extra photograph is the only asset in the listing that no other seller has.

## What was deliberately not done

No keyword was repeated to hit a density target. No review stars were added, because the product has no reviews yet, and no structured data claiming ratings was added either. The word count roughly tripled, but only because there were three real things left unanswered, not because length was the goal.

## How to judge the rewrite

Record clicks, impressions and average position for the 28 days before the change. Change the page once, note the date, and leave it alone. Compare after four weeks.

If impressions rise but clicks do not, the title needs work. If clicks rise but sales do not, the page is promising something the product does not deliver. If nothing changes at all, the honest conclusion is usually that nobody is searching for this design, and the fix is a better design or a different channel, not more words.
